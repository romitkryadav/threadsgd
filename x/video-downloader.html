Which Link Should You Copy?

Use the post link, not the profile link — this trips up a lot of users.

  • Profile link looks like x.com/username. It opens someone's page, not a specific post. The tool has no way to know which video you want from that.
  • Post link looks like x.com/username/status/1234567890. That long number identifies a single post. If you see /status/ in the URL, you're on the right track.
  • Old Twitter links use twitter.com/username/status/1234567890 — these work exactly the same, no changes needed.

A couple of edge cases: if someone quote-posted a video, the video belongs to the original post — open that one and copy its link. And if you copied a link from a search result or someone's timeline, open the post itself first. The safest way to get a clean link is to tap the share icon on the post and choose Copy link — copying from the address bar is where links tend to get cut off.

Downloading on Android and iPhone

The tool works in your phone's browser the same way it does on a desktop. In the X app, tap the share button on the post, tap Copy Link, then open the link in your browser and use this tool.

  • Android: Downloaded videos usually go to the Downloads folder. Check the Files app first; some devices also show them in Gallery or Photos.
  • iPhone: Use Safari. A download icon appears near the address bar when it finishes. The file lands in Downloads inside the Files app. To move it to your camera roll, open the video, tap Share, and select Save Video.

If you can't find the download, check your browser's own downloads section — its location varies by browser and settings.

MP4 and Video Quality

Videos on X are stored as MP4s, and that's the format you get here — no conversion happens. If a post has multiple quality options, they're all listed so you can choose. The largest file is usually the best quality. Keep in mind the quality cap is set by the original upload — the tool can't give you resolution that wasn't there to begin with. If you need a format other than MP4, you'll have to convert the file separately after downloading.

Make Sure the Post Actually Has a Video

A post can contain a photo, a GIF, a link preview, or plain text — this tool is for videos only. If the post has a photo, try the X Image Downloader. For a profile picture, use the X DP Downloader. To check someone's follower count, the X Follower Counter has you covered.

Watch out for content that looks like a video but isn't — a YouTube embed or a news site link preview is not a video hosted on X, so there's nothing to extract. GIFs on X are actually short looping MP4 clips and will download as video files.

Public, Protected, and Deleted Posts

This tool works with public posts only — ones you can open without logging in. If an account is protected, its posts are visible only to approved followers and can't be accessed here. The tool doesn't bypass privacy settings, and it shouldn't. If it's your own protected account, the original video should already be on your device or wherever you uploaded it from.

Deleted posts are gone. Once a post or video is removed, no downloader can bring it back. If you see an old post cached somewhere, it may just be a local memory. Try reaching out to the original creator.

Video Not Downloading?

Just because a post is visible in the app doesn't always mean the video can be extracted. Some posts require login before the video loads, or the file may have restrictions X applies on their end. Retrying repeatedly won't fix those cases. Before giving up, check the following:

  • You copied a profile, search page, or timeline link. None of those point to a specific video — open the post and copy from there.
  • The link is incomplete. It needs to end with the long number after /status/. Copy it again using the share button.
  • The post doesn't contain a video — it might be a photo, GIF, or link preview.
  • The post was deleted or the account is private. See the section above.
  • The video opened in a new tab instead of downloading. Use the video player's three-dot menu, or long-press the video and choose download.

If everything checks out — correct link, public post, video confirmed — and it still fails, the video source may not be available to the tool at that moment. Try again later.

Before You Reuse a Video

Videos posted on X belong to the people who made them. Downloading for personal viewing is one thing. Reposting, including in your own content, or using commercially is another matter entirely. If you plan to use a video beyond your own viewing, get permission from the original creator first.
