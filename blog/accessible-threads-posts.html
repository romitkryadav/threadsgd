Accessibility

How to Make Threads Posts More Accessible

Accessibility starts with clear language and useful context. Learn how to describe images, review media alternatives, and make posts easier to follow.

ThreadsGrab guide · Updated November 20, 2026

Make the main point clear in text

Write the key information in the post rather than placing every important detail only inside an image or video. Use familiar words, define specialist terms when needed, and break long explanations into readable sections. If the post refers to a chart or photo, explain what readers should notice.

Decorative symbols and long emoji strings can make text harder for some people using assistive technology. Use emoji when they add meaning, and do not rely on color alone to distinguish important information.

Describe images with useful alt text

Alt text should convey the information a person needs from an image. For a photo of a finished ceramic bowl, a concise description might say, “Blue glazed bowl with a wide rim on a wooden table.” If the image is a chart, describe its central comparison or include the key numbers in the post text as well.

Avoid repeating the caption word for word when the caption already explains the same thing. Skip phrases such as “image of” unless they help the sentence make sense. Meta’s Threads launch announcement notes the ability to review and edit automatically generated alt text for photos and videos; the controls shown may vary as the app changes. See the Threads accessibility and feature overview.

Give video viewers context

If a clip contains spoken instructions, names, or other essential details, include that information in the post text or provide captions when the current composer offers them. Check that captions are understandable and correctly represent the speech; automatic captions can mishear names, technical terms, and accents.

For a silent visual clip, a short description can explain the action. Do not assume every viewer can hear narration or see small text embedded in the video.

Keep links and formatting understandable

Tell readers what a link leads to instead of writing “click here” without context. Use paragraphs and punctuation to make the message easy to scan. If you share a screenshot containing text, consider adding the important wording in the post or in a text attachment so readers do not have to zoom into the image.

Stylized Unicode fonts may look decorative but can be difficult for screen readers or may render differently across devices. Keep names, instructions, and key details in standard text. If you use stylized characters for a heading or bio, check that the plain meaning remains understandable.

A quick accessibility check before posting

  • Can someone understand the purpose of the post from its text?
  • Does the image description explain relevant content rather than its decoration?
  • Can the video’s important spoken information be read?
  • Are links described clearly?
  • Would the post still make sense without its color, sound, or decorative typeface?

Small checks like these can make a post more usable without changing its voice. Accessibility is not a separate style; it is part of sharing information clearly.
