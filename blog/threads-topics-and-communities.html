How to Use Topics and Communities on Threads

A practical look at choosing relevant topics, participating in communities, and making your posts easier for the right people to discover.

ThreadsGrab guide · Updated November 20, 2026

Threads conversations are easier to find when they connect clearly to an interest. Topics and communities can help organize that discovery, but they work best when they describe what a post is actually about. Adding a label simply to reach a larger audience can confuse readers and make the discussion less useful.

What topics and communities do

A topic gives a post a subject that readers may browse or follow. Communities group people around shared interests so they can discover discussions and take part. Availability and features can vary as Meta rolls them out. Metaâ€™s announcement describes Threads communities as interest-based spaces for conversations; check the app for the options currently available to your account.

Choose a topic that fits the post

Before choosing a topic, summarize your post in a few words. If you are sharing a beginnerâ€™s gardening tip, a gardening topic is a better match than a broad trending category. If a post touches several subjects, select the one a reader would most expect to discuss in replies. Skip the label when none fits.

Relevance helps set expectations. Someone who opens a topic should find posts that belong there, not unrelated promotions. A specific, accurate topic can also make your contribution easier to understand when it appears outside your followersâ€™ feeds.

Participate before promoting

In a community, read a few recent discussions before posting. Notice whether members prefer questions, personal experience, project updates, or detailed answers. Add something useful in that style: share what you tried, explain a result, or ask a focused question that invites more than a yes-or-no reply.

Community participation is not a shortcut to automatic reach. People may see your contribution, but they decide whether it is relevant enough to read or respond to. Avoid repeating the same link or sales pitch across unrelated spaces. Follow each communityâ€™s posted rules and the platformâ€™s current policies.

Write for a real conversation

Give the post enough context to make sense on its own. Instead of â€œThoughts?â€?, explain the decision you are weighing and what you have already considered. A clear question helps readers answer from their own experience. If you share a photo or video, add a sentence explaining why it matters to the topic.

Review what readers respond to

After posting, look beyond raw views. Did people ask follow-up questions? Did the conversation stay on topic? Were replies helpful? Those signals can tell you whether your framing gave the community something worth discussing. Use Threads Insights if it is available to you, while remembering that metrics describe activity and do not fully explain why someone responded.

A simple example

Suppose you are sharing a home coffee experiment. Choose a coffee-related topic if one is available, state what you changed, and include the result. Then ask a specific question such as which grind setting others would test next. The label helps interested readers find the subject; the detail gives them a reason to join in.

Use topics and communities as signposts for genuine participation. Accurate labels, useful context, and considerate replies create a better experience for both the people who find your post and the people already taking part.
