Writing and publishing

Threads Character Limit: 500 Characters and Longer Text Attachments

Threads posts have a short main text field, while a longer text attachment can give some posts more room. Here is how to plan around both and check the current composer.

ThreadsGrab guide · Updated November 20, 2026

How long can a Threads post be?

Meta’s original Threads announcement described posts of up to 500 characters. In September 2025, Meta announced an option to attach up to 10,000 characters of text to a post. Availability and the composer experience can change, so check the options shown in your account before planning around a feature.

The 500-character main post is useful for a focused update, question, or introduction to a longer idea. A text attachment can carry more context when the main post alone would be too compressed. Meta’s announcement explains the longer-text feature in its Threads text attachment update.

Choose between a short post and an attachment

Keep the main post short when one idea can stand on its own. Put the essential point first, then add only the context readers need to understand it. A clear short post is easier to scan and reply to than a paragraph cut down until its meaning disappears.

Consider a longer attachment when you need to explain a sequence, share a substantial reflection, or provide background that would be hard to fit in the main text. Use the opening post to introduce the subject and tell readers what the attached text contains. Do not make the attachment longer simply because there is room; length should serve the reader.

Example: instead of squeezing a project update into one dense block, write a concise opening that states what changed, then attach the detail readers may want to explore.

A simple editing pass

  1. Write the complete thought first. Draft without watching the counter so you do not remove context prematurely.
  2. Find the main point. Put the conclusion, question, or update near the beginning.
  3. Remove repetition. Delete duplicate setup, filler phrases, and details that do not support the point.
  4. Break up dense text. Use short paragraphs and concrete wording when the composer allows it.
  5. Check the final draft in Threads. Paste it into the composer and use the count or attachment options displayed there as the final check.

What counts as a character?

Spaces, punctuation, line breaks, links, and combined emoji can be treated differently by different counters. A tool may count visible symbols in a way that is close to, but not identical to, the Threads composer. If a draft is near the limit, leave a little room and confirm it in the app before publishing.

The Threads Character Counter can help you check a draft as you edit. It is a planning aid, not an official Threads validator, so treat the composer’s displayed count as the final check.

Do not trade clarity for a lower count

Abbreviations, missing punctuation, and unexplained shorthand can make a post harder to understand. If a detail matters, use the longer text option when it is available, or publish a short post that links to a fuller explanation. Make the destination clear before asking readers to open a link.

Before publishing, read the post once as someone who does not know the background. If the point or next step is unclear, revise the wording rather than only trimming characters.
