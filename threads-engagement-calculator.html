Calculate a Threads Engagement Rate

Use this Threads engagement rate calculator to see how a post's interactions compare with its audience or its views. Enter the likes, replies, reposts, and quotes, then add the follower count to calculate engagement by followers. If you have a view or impression count, the tool calculates that rate separately.

The calculator uses this formula: (likes + replies + reposts + quotes) ÷ followers or views × 100. For example, 120 interactions divided by 4,000 followers gives a 3% follower-based rate. Using 2,000 views as the denominator gives a 6% view-based rate. Keep the denominator clear when you compare or share results.

Engagement by Followers and Engagement by Views

A follower-based rate compares interactions with the account's follower count. A view-based rate compares those interactions with a post's views or impressions. These rates answer different questions and should not be treated as interchangeable. If you use impressions, label the result as impression-based in your notes.

Views or impressions may not be available for every post. Leave that field blank if you do not have a reliable count; the calculator will still show the follower-based rate. A zero or missing denominator cannot produce a meaningful percentage.

Use Counts from the Same Post or Period

For a single-post calculation, use that post's interactions and the follower or view count you want to compare them with. For a reporting period, total the interactions for that same period and apply a consistent denominator. Avoid combining one post's interactions with view counts from a different post or time window.

What the Calculator Includes

The interaction total includes likes, replies, reposts, and quotes. It does not add other actions such as profile visits or link clicks. Counts are entered manually, so you can use figures from a post or report you already have without giving the tool access to your account.

Use Your Own Baseline

There is no single engagement percentage that defines a successful Threads post for every account. Audience size, content, and the measurement method all affect the result. Compare posts using the same interactions, denominator, and reporting window to understand changes in your own account.
